## SPINE

**A man too slow for the fight survives it.** An arrow misses the eye, a second goes into a hand, a fire spell buys a blink, and the staff takes an eye. He swings and misses, falls, and is pinned. The beast kills itself driving its tusks down onto the staff he braces. Then he reaches the woman in the dark, and it is his wife.

## What this scene needed that a base form has no field for

1. **Negative space without dialogue.** Every unsaid thing is the narrator's, and the body carries it. The field holds, but its "carried by" column fills with physical detail instead of lines.
2. **Sentence length against the expectation.** A drafter's reflex for a fight is short sentences. This fight runs longer than the book's average, with short paragraphs held back for the turns. It is not true of all his action: the Rift's pishacha attacks run short (`SYNTHESIS.md`, RHYTHM). The map needs a field for rhythm when a scene departs from the book's norm, and which direction.
3. **A capability stated at its limit before it is used.** *"An adequate marksman, but he had never been fast"* (`:1011`) comes before either arrow. The win doesn't come from a capability at all; the beast kills itself. CAPABILITIES needs each ability's limit, and the map needs to say what actually decides the outcome.
4. **A non-human's intent, inferred.** Moves 5 and 6 read the rakshasa's mind from its body: it doesn't realize it has him, then it does (`:1023`–`1025`). Close third on Toril, with the monster's state readable through what it does.
5. **An object with a history used as a weapon.** The staff of Kelun, which Toril took from his father on his wedding day (`:336`–`405`), goes through the rakshasa's skull. OBJECTS needs to record an object's use as well as its meaning.
