Dialogue share is measured with `tools/style_fingerprint.py`'s rules (words inside straight quotes). Cordimancy's 110 scenes have a median of 31% dialogue and a median length of 983 words; nine scenes have none and twelve more have under 5% (the rakshasa fight, the wolves, Malena alone in Kikal Pilar, the climb out of the Rift). The sample is biased twice: I chose relationship scenes, so no magic is used in any of them, and only one near-silent scene is mapped (ch 9, added after the first seven).

## Kept from the Deep Resonance form

- **PLACE, TIME, PRESENT, BODY, WANTS, CLOCK** — used by all seven. CLOCK in ch 4 is "nothing presses", which is itself worth recording.
- **SENSES** — ordinary human senses, but three scenes isolate one sense on purpose: the daggers heard one by one (ch 7), the captors present only as boots and oars (ch 38), the lilies Toril can't see (ch 24). Keep, and ask which sense carries whom.
- **PRESENT, with what each presence does.** The servants in ch 2 raise the cost of every line, and the men on the bank in ch 51 are why Malena screams. Extend PRESENT to say what a witness does to the scene.
- **RELATIONS** — all seven move a tie to someone absent: Toril in ch 4 and ch 2, Hasha in ch 7, Paka in ch 51, Toril's parents in ch 47, Maco in ch 38, Nanar in ch 24.
- **REGARD** — all seven, extended to the scene's antagonist where there is one: ch 2's father gets his best moment just before the refusal. That's the brief's *"trapped, not deeply evil"* (`plan/brief.md`) applied per scene.
- **TEXTURE**, **READER** — all seven. READER carries ch 47 entirely (Malena's pregnancy, known from her own POV, `:2431`–`2432`), under the ruling that rotation is a legitimate source of irony (Q-XV7A).
- **PAST IN PLAY**, widened from Deep Resonance's constructed identities to any memory a character measures the present against: Toril's parents after Amar's death (ch 47), Shivi's basket (ch 51), Malena's naming day (ch 4), the ocarina (ch 2).
- **LEXICON**, widened twice. Per speaker, not only per POV: Oji's compound verbs and *"stonecaster"* (ch 24). And per register: Kinora's child's words and her kitchen-girl simile (ch 38), and her *"Semya ur-Hasha"* and *"Tat"* (ch 4).

## Rarely used

- **WEATHER** — given in passing in four of seven, and doing work only in ch 51 (cold water). Keep it, but it may be blank.
- **CHANNELS** — only ch 7 (the shimsal). Keep it for Voices and proxies.
- **CAPABILITIES** — none of the seven. Keep it anyway: The Clanless runs on aiki, the staff and kindling, and the sample is biased against them.

## Dropped

- **ELSEWHERE** — Deep Resonance's second world. The Clanless has one world.

## New

1. **SUBTITLE.** Every one of Cordimancy's 56 chapters opens *"topic ~ POV"* in italics (`:36`, `:197` …), with a time note only for the jump at ch 2. The header needs it, or a drafter drops the device.
2. **NEGATIVE SPACE** (shape approved, Q-ZN70). In all seven. Each item: what is unsaid; by a character or by the narrator; what carries it instead; where or whether it is paid; the sentence a drafter must not write. A character's withholding goes here with its reason, as Toril's prudence at the council does (Q-B8DE). Rule: in a scene with talk, carved out of talk, never substituted for it (`~/code/me/novel-method/tensions.md`, `withholding`). The seven run from 24% to 71% dialogue, and even the lowest, ch 38 and ch 47, turn on what is said aloud.
3. **PRESSURE.** What is being held down, since which scene, and how many times. Toril's anger is swallowed three times before it breaks (ch 7); his defense is held in narration and never spoken (ch 47); Malena flushes three times and never protests (ch 2). The release lands because the reader has been counting, and the count runs across scenes, so a map has to carry it in.
4. **LABELS AND NAMES.** Which label is pinned on whom, and which name is held against it. *"Boy"* against *"chief of Kelun"* (ch 7); *"But I'm a good worker"* (ch 4); *"a dolt of a girl"*, which Malena pins on herself (ch 51, Q-CR18). The brief's theme (`plan/brief.md`, "Labels are imposed; names are chosen and held") runs through single scenes, and a map should name its rung.
5. **OBJECTS.** Each object that carries meaning, and what it means to each person present, because the meanings can be opposite: the heartstone (ch 47), the signet and the daisy (ch 2), Toril's rooms and slings (ch 4), the lilies (ch 24). An absent character can be built entirely from objects (ch 4).
6. **TRUTH.** Which behavior from the character's truth profile the scene enacts: Shivi refusing two lies at once (ch 51), Toril withholding until he knows who's complicit (ch 7), Malena catastrophizing her guilt (ch 51). This makes the profiles in `plan/truth-profiles/` load-bearing in every map, not background.
7. **MIND** (for a child POV, and possibly for any POV). Not only how the POV speaks but how they reason: Kinora treats a wish as evidence and asks as though asking could work (ch 38). The failure to name is adult insight in a child's head.

## Move tags the maps needed

- `[refusal by body]` — stillness, a boot, eyes that won't meet (ch 2, ch 24, ch 4).
- `[refrain]` — a repeated line that carries the action: *"Shivi kept wading"* (ch 51), *"Oji waited"* (ch 24).
- `[story for argument]` — a character answers with a story that persuades where argument wouldn't (ch 51).
- `[shown, not argued]` — an object or a sense makes the point (ch 24).
- `[comic — structural]` — humor that delivers exposition or relief (ch 2's Tupa, ch 4's blackberry tarts). Daniel: *"I worked hard on those."*
- `[misread]` — the POV gets something wrong in a way the reader can decode: Malena expecting Toril (ch 51), Toril's *"simple courtesy"* (ch 47).
- `[interior — two voices]` — the POV argues with himself and speaks from one side (ch 47).
- `[register against content]` — said flatly, felt deeply (ch 4's Kinora); threats getting quieter as they get worse (ch 7's shimsal).
- `[the wrong rescuer]` — who the POV expects and who comes (ch 51).

## What should become a layer, not a field

PRESSURE and LABELS AND NAMES both run across scenes. A field in one map can't count what happened three scenes earlier, so each needs a book-grain owner, a layer that tracks the count or the rung and that each map cites. OBJECTS that travel, like the acorn, the antechild's shards and Cricket's name, belong in A7's threads (`~/code/me/novel-method/runbook.md` A7, "Carries threads").

## What the eighth map added (ch 9, the rakshasa)

- **NEGATIVE SPACE holds without dialogue.** Every unsaid thing is the narrator's, and the body carries it: one named feeling in the whole fight, then wrists, lungs and grunts.
- **RHYTHM, when a scene departs from the book's norm.** The rakshasa fight's narration sentences average 17.3 words against 12.2 book-wide. Daniel asked whether that holds across his action scenes (Q-NFD8), and it doesn't. Of eleven action scenes, the long-sentence ones are the one-on-one fights where a body is grappling a body: the rakshasa (17.3), the wolf on Malena (14.9), the log hitting the boulder (14.2). The Rift sequences run short: the chase to the wall (9.1), both pishacha attacks (10.0, 10.2), the climb (10.2). Those are group scenes in blinding mist, with the enemy mostly heard rather than seen (*"Whoops, shrieks, and grunts followed,"* `:3682`). The palace climax sits at the book's norm (12.9). Within the wolves scene, sentences before and after contact are the same length (15.5 against 14.9), so it isn't contact alone. Daniel: drafting era isn't the explanation, and the split *"isn't deliberate craft choice, but rather intuition on my part"* (Q-GXBE). So it is a pattern to describe to a drafter, not a rule he holds: long jointed sentences where a body is grappling a body and events pile up faster than they can be separated; short fragments where the POV perceives a group fight in poor visibility, one sound at a time. A map should record a scene's rhythm and say which of the two modes it is in.
- **What decides the outcome.** Toril's limits are stated before he uses his skills, and the beast kills itself on the braced staff. The map should say what wins, separately from CAPABILITIES.
- **OBJECTS records use as well as meaning.** The staff of Kelun is a spear here.
