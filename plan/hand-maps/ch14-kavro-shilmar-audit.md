## How it differs

1. **The god doesn't contract.** Gitám uses 7.5 contractions per thousand words, against 46 for Toril and 47 for the book's dialogue. The narration says his voice is *"deep and quiet"* and different from the priest's own (`:1404`). The missing contractions are the measurable part of that difference: *"We do not dismiss those who want to hear truth"* (`:1451`), *"Speakers cannot choose for you"* (`:1430`).
2. **The student asks; the god states.** Toril's sentences are questions twice as often as the book's dialogue (36% against 18%); Gitám's are questions less often (15%). But Gitám's few questions carry the scene: *"Tell me your name"*, *"What additional name would you hold?"*, *"Who will you be?"*, *"Does love demand that Malena take the name 'Healed-by-Toril' so you can have the name you want?"* (`:1404`, `:1406`, `:1432`, `:1490`). He refuses to answer twice: *"I will let you judge"* (`:1439`), and silence (`:1462`, `:1500`).
3. **One word, repeated.** Gitám says *name* or a form of it forty times in 81 sentences. The scene never reaches for a synonym, and the repetition is what lets the aphorisms land: *"With people, all true names are self-names"* (`:1445`).
4. **Toril's interior is a stream of questions.** Narration asks 12.9 questions per thousand words, more than twice the book's rate: *"Was that it? Had he failed?"* (`:1457`), *"Was this silence a test?"* (`:1460`). It is the one scene where the POV's uncertainty is the texture of the prose.
5. **A scene inside the scene.** The parable of First Man (`:1414`–`1432`) is told in Gitám's own first person, unquoted: *"Many times he asked one of me, but I refused."* First Man mirrors Toril: he names his laugh a *"mistake"*, and Gitám asks whether that name shapes his soul (`:1426`). Later Gitám says it outright: *"How like First Man you are, child!"* (`:1484`), and Toril remembers *"First Man admitting the inaccuracy of his word 'mistake'"* (`:1493`). The parable is the key, handed over before the lock.
6. **Light is feedback.** A partial name brings a rose-colored pinpoint, then darkness (`:1456`). The step into the water brings a faint outline (`:1482`). The right question brings the priest's face flaring too bright to look at (`:1502`). The prose says nothing about what the light means. The reader learns to read it.
7. **The choice is a step, not a word.** The turn of the scene is Toril stepping into black water he can't see (`:1475`). *"Well done,"* and then: *"I could not choose to step for you; that would make it my name"* (`:1481`).
8. **Hyphenated names as labels.** *Healer-of-Malena*, *Healed-by-Toril*, *Fails-at-Everything-that-Matters*, *seeker-of-the-helpless* (`:1455`, `:1490`, `:1499`, `:1505`). The scene is the book's theme in its purest form, and Toril catastrophizes here exactly as Malena does at the river (`:4303`; ruling, Q-CR18): *"Is that my name— Fails-at-Everything-that-Matters?"*
9. **Verse in an unglossed language.** The invocation (`:1390`–`1393`) is four italic lines that the narration never translates. It joins *Bulen zhová!* (`:1016`) on the list of canon words missing from `kb/worldbuilding/lingo.md`.

## Negative space

- **What Gitám withholds** is the scene's method: the answer Toril wants, and his presence for hours (`:1462`–`1465`). The silence is a test, and the narration doesn't say so; Toril guesses at it (`:1460`).
- **The name, until the end.** The reader waits through the whole ordeal for the name Toril swept on his naming day (`:176`–`186`).
- **Canon's own use of the term.** Toril found his first name in negative space. On his naming day he reads the glyph in his broom strokes *"once he focused on the negative space he'd unwittingly created"* (`:178`). Daniel's concept is in the book as the way its hero first learns who he is.

## Ruled

Gitám's uncontracted register is *"quite deliberate"*. *"Other Speakers might not speak like Gitam, though. That may be idiolect."* (Q-ZG39) So it belongs to Gitám's LEXICON entry, not to a rule for divine speech.

## What this adds to the form

- **LEXICON** needs register as well as vocabulary: contractions, formality, a single repeated word. Gitám is the clearest case.
- **A nested scene.** A story told inside a scene that the scene then turns on. A map needs to record the nested story and which of its moves the outer scene repeats.
- **Feedback channels.** Light here plays the part a reaction plays between people. The map needs to record a non-verbal signal the reader is taught to read.
