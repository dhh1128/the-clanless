## What this scene needed that a base form has no field for

1. **An absent character built from objects.** Toril is never on the page, and the scene is about him: rooms, books, slings, boots, a rocking chair, a daisy, a child he hired. The map needs a field for which objects carry an absent person, and what each one says.
2. **Two wounds side by side, uncompared.** Moves 7 and 8 set Malena's naming-day grievance next to Kinora's matter-of-fact account of being hit. The prose never draws the comparison. A map needs to record a juxtaposition the drafter must leave unspoken.
3. **A child's history told flatly.** Kinora reports her stepfather's rough hand *"so matter-of-fact"* that Malena turns to look at her face (`:569`). The feeling is in the gap between the voice and the eyes. The map needs to say when a character's register runs against what they're saying.
4. **Trust built through a small transaction.** Malena asks for help instead of offering it (`:525`), covers for the forgotten lamps (`:535`), and accepts help with her hair (`:539`). The relationship moves through exchanges, not declarations, which RELATIONS should name move by move.
5. **The comic in a sad scene.** The blackberry tarts and the cup of honey (`:577`) land directly after the stepfather. As in ch 2, humor does structural work, here as relief that keeps the scene from turning bleak (`plan/brief.md`, "It should not be bleak").
