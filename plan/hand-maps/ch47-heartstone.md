## SPINE

**A husband asks for friendship and offers a marriage.** Toril gets Malena alone, asks about *"us"*, and listens. She offers to try to be friends, and he is moved to tears. Then he gives her the heartstone, which means the one thing she has just told him she can't give, and she throws it away.

## What this scene needed that a base form has no field for

1. **The reader knows what the POV doesn't.** The scene's irony depends on Malena's POV at `:2431`–`2432`, which Toril has never had. The rotating POV supplies it. This contradicted an earlier `[?]` clause in `AGENTS.md` §6, since dropped (ruling, Q-XV7A): rotation is a legitimate source of irony.
2. **The past in play, as a model.** Toril's memory of his parents (`:4047`–`4048`) is what he is reaching for. The map needs to record which memory a character is measuring the present against.
3. **Two interior voices.** *"A part of him rebelled … Another part of Toril's heart was a harsher critic"* (`:4032`–`4033`). The map needs to record when a POV argues with himself, and which voice he speaks from.
4. **The right gift at the wrong moment.** The heartstone is an act of love and an injury at once. The map needs to say what an object means to each person, because here the meanings are opposite.
5. **Trauma carried by the body.** *"Her eyes had gone somewhere else"* (`:4075`) calls back the dagger. As in ch 38 and ch 24, the body carries what nobody can say.
