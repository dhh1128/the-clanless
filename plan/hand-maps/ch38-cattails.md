## A child's mind, not only a child's voice

Kinora doesn't only talk like a child; she reasons like one, and the narration stays inside that reasoning without correcting it.

- **Wishing as evidence.** *"Whoever is coming has to be close. They have to be."* (`:3130`)
- **Asking as if it could work.** She asks a catatonic toddler to promise to make noise (`:3132`, `:3134`), and pleads with him (*"Please"*) as though his silence were a choice.
- **Concrete stakes.** The danger is the barge, *"floating away down the river"* (`:3130`), not death or slavery in the abstract.
- **No moral framing of herself.** She never thinks of what she's doing as brave or as a sacrifice. She thinks about what has to happen next.
- **A help she can't name.** *"You need someone big to help you. I can't tell what's wrong."* (`:3132`) She knows the limits of what she understands.

A drafter's failure here is the adult insight put in a child's mouth or head: Kinora reflecting on her own courage, or the narration explaining what the choice means.

## What this scene needed that a base form has no field for

1. **Dialogue to someone who can't answer.** Every line is a request to a mute toddler, and the requests change shape: a blink, a small blink, bravery, a noise, *"Please"*. A map needs to record a one-sided exchange and how the ask escalates, since a drafter will otherwise give Cricket a response.
2. **A costly choice never named.** The scene's central decision, that she goes back so he isn't missed, is never said. The reader infers it from one practical sentence (`:3132`). The map needs a field for a decision the POV makes without stating it, with the line the reader infers it from.
3. **A child's reasoning, in a child's words.** *"Bad men," "someone big," "Can you do that for me?"* (`:3132`) The POV's vocabulary and logic are a child's, and the narration stays inside them. LEXICON has to cover register and age, not only names.
4. **Competence in a child.** Testing his breathing by covering his mouth (`:3122`) and reading where the guards are (`:3127`) are not childish. Kinora is *"very intelligent, child-naive, but quick to see and learn"* (`plan/truth-profiles/kinora-and-tupa.md`). REGARD needs the specific competent act.
5. **Captors as sound.** The soldiers are boots, grunts and oars. No soldier is described or named. The map needs to record which characters are present only through one sense.
6. **A short scene with its paired scene elsewhere.** It depends on ch 31 for the name and the bond, and it's paid four times later (`:3450`, `:3525`, `:4170`, `:4680`). As in the other maps, links to earlier and later scenes carry much of the weight.
