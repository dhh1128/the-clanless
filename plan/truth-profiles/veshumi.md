# Truth profile: Veshumi

Each claim is marked *canon*, with a line in `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (cordimancy commit `64b2f69`), or *proposal*, which waits for his correction. Canon says very little about her, so nearly everything here is proposal. A profile makes her consistent in her own eyes. It does not by that alone make her someone a reader likes, so that is handled separately below.

## What canon gives her

- She is the eldest of the three Shavir daughters. She has been married six years to cement the family's ties to Rovin, and has no child. Her father: *"Our oldest has had six years to produce issue to cement our ties to Rovin. And nothing to show for it."* (`:225`). Her mother: *"Veshumi is still trying."* (`:224`). See `plan/backstory.md` SH3.
- In her family, childbirth kills women: the grandmother and the aunt (`:224`; SH2).
- Her father's life's work was restoring the Shavir line's standing: *"It's been twelve generations since the line of Shavir held the staff of clan chief; you can restore us to the status we deserve."* (`:225`). Her mother wore the *"gaudy ruby"* and the loneliness that came with it (`:4116`).
- Her parents are dead and never found (SH1).
- She is married to Rovin's cousin: *"My sister's married to his cousin, Toril."* (`:1710`). Canon doesn't name him. He is also Rovin's protégé, faithful to her and emotionally absent (ruling, Q-DEFS; `plan/truth-profiles/rovin.md`).

## Two kinds of struggle

- **The conscious dilemma: her sisters, or her place.** A wife with no child, in a marriage made for alliance, holds her place only while she stays useful to her husband's house. Her husband is Rovin's protégé, so taking Malena in, or siding with her, means setting herself against her husband's patron while Rovin is turning against Toril. She knows exactly what each choice costs, and she is torn, the way Malena is torn over telling Toril.
- **The confident blind spot: respectability is safety.** She is sure the way to survive is to be beyond reproach. It was their father's creed, and with him dead she carries it as a duty to him, without noticing that it is his voice speaking. The reader can see it is the cage Malena escaped. She is also partly right: in Sotalio, in winter, under Rovin, respectability really is protection, and for an eleven-year-old it may be the only protection there is. This is the mirror of Malena's blind spot about Tupa. Each sister is certain she is the one protecting the youngest.

## How she lies to herself (proposal)

Her harshness about the pregnancy feels to her like prudence: *someone in this family has to think about how this looks.* Underneath it are three things she won't let herself feel. The first is terror, because women in their line die in childbirth (SH2), and the acceleration makes it worse. The second is six years of grief over trying. The third is envy, which she would be ashamed to own. The essay describes this mechanism: an emotion that is felt sincerely and still lies, because it justifies an act (`~/code/me/sivanea.com/on-lying-and-truth-telling.md`, "We can lie with our emotions"). Her contempt protects her from her fear.

## Labels (proposal)

- She calls herself *the dutiful one*, the daughter who did what was asked.
- She calls Malena *reckless*.
- She is called *childless* and worse, in whispers. Never use "barren" in the plan.

## How she bends truth, and how she tells it (proposal)

- **Bends it through normalcy:** *this is how things are done.*
- **Minimizes her own grief:** she has no time for it.
- **Tells it as a practical blow nobody else will say aloud.** She is the one who tells Malena she could die having this child, the way their aunt did. It is brutal, it is true, and it is the most loving thing anyone in Sotalio says to Malena. Malena hears only the cruelty.

## What makes her someone the reader can like, not just someone who makes sense (proposal)

1. **She took their father's pressure first.** Six years older than Malena, or thereabouts, she was the daughter he spent his ambition on before Malena. She married where she was told, and that bought Malena some of the freedom Malena never knew she was given.
2. **She shares the family wit.** It is the same dry, sharp humor Malena has (*"Heaven's Fist! How many conversational firecrackers…"*, `:214`), turned on the town instead of on herself. The reader enjoys her company before they understand her.
3. **She cares for Tupa practically.** Gemini has her buy Tupa "a gaudy dress". Instead she gives a warm cloak, does Tupa's hair, makes sure Tupa eats.
4. **She helps in secret at the gate.** When the exiles leave, supplies for the road reach them and nobody admits to sending them. Malena's first kindness to Toril in Cordimancy was anonymous too, so the reader sees that the sisters are more alike than either knows.
5. **She comes to the birth, and stays.** This is a ruling and reverses the Gemini plan, in which she spurns Malena. She knows the family's history with childbirth better than anyone, and she comes anyway. She says the wrong things the whole time, and she stays.

**The constraint on that ruling.** The book is about chosen family and belonging, and its title names the cost. *"We have to be surgical about the fact that Tupa and Malena's choice essentially make them clanless. It's that 'essentially' that gives us a little freedom -- but the feelings of isolation need to be real. Veshumi's own choice is not to become clanless, and it should be understandable, and heart-rending because we understand her."*

- **Hazard:** the birth softens the exile into a family quarrel that will mend. If the reader expects Veshumi to follow them to the Rift, the isolation was never real. She came because she is their sister. She does not leave, and the birth is what makes her not leaving hurt.
- **Hazard:** her secret supplies at the gate (item 4) are read as reconciliation. They are her goodbye. They stay anonymous, and Malena may never be sure who sent them.

## The contested choice she believes is right (proposal)

She fights to keep Tupa in Sotalio. She believes it is the only safe place for a child in winter, and that Malena is choosing her own pride over their sister's survival. The reader may disagree. They should also be unable to say she is simply wrong.
