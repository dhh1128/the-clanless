# Truth profiles: Kinora and Tupa (worked together)

They are handled together because Daniel suggested it: *"talking about her and Tupa together may be worth doing."* Each claim cites `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (cordimancy commit `64b2f69`), or quotes Daniel, or is marked `[?]` as the session's brainstorm.

## Daniel's statements

- *"Kinora is traumatized. She's been through terrible things. Traumatized children have to overcome much. Tupa is also traumatized BTW, but differently; instead of a brutish and physically abusive stepfather, she's had a father who was shallow and self-interested and a poor husband to her mother. Both of these girls have to go through healing journeys, with missteps."*
- *"Kinora has a nurturing heart (as her actions with Cricket prove), and is brave in some ways, but needs to feel some stability and needs to learn to trust adults. Tupa already trusts Malena, and she may also have some connection to Veshumi as well."*
- *"Both of these girls are about to reach the age where they choose a self-name, and this may figure in the storyline."*
- *"They also know one another, and some of their traumatic experiences were shared. So they may have a trauma bond. They may compete a little for Malena or Toril's affection. They may feel much solidarity with each other."*
- *"both end up becoming the big sisters to the littler orphans that Toril and Malena adopt, and both will be protective big sisters to little Paka."*
- *"In the end, I see Kinora being a bright flame, personality-wise, while Tupa becomes a warm coal. Both beautiful, but different."*
- *"Possibly one of them becomes sata, as Toril vowed and failed, and thus lives to write the history we've been reading? Possibly one becomes Osipi? Or possibly none of this, and they are both ordinary humans living good lives."*
- *"Kinora does become a clan leader (Toril's successor)."*

## Canon

- **Naming day comes at about twelve.** Malena: *"As a shivering twelve-year-old, she'd searched the rocks until she found 'beauty' and 'scholar'"* (`:4519`). Toril was *"barely past my naming day"* at thirteen (`:2329`).
- **Tupa:** eleven when Cordimancy opens (`:214`). Her hair *"was always scraggly unless Malena attended to it"* (`:716`). She was afraid of the fosterage her father had arranged with *Erim's* household: *"I don't know anybody in Erim's household"* (`:731`–`737`). Malena asked her *"Do you trust me?"* and promised they would talk (`:741`–`743`). Shivi took her place under Gorumim's knife: *"It was supposed to be me, but it was grandma instead"* (`:4734`). She carries Shivi's acorn and her charge, *"you'd know what to do with it now"* (`:4750`).
- **Kinora:** she *"looked to be perhaps seven or eight years old"* to Malena when they met (`:520`). Her real father, her *tat*, was killed by a sloth bear when she was a baby. He taught her the hunters' and shepherds' quattroglyphs on *"happy meanders on the hillsides"* (`:563`, `:2497`). Her stepfather *"has a rough hand"* (`:567`). Her stepbrother is Elesel, who is mean, and her younger brother is Maco, four years old (`:565`). She risked a beating to leave a quattroglyph as a trail for rescuers (`:2485`–`2497`). She hid Cricket in the rushes because *"He was not going to make it"* (`:4680`), and her first question when she was rescued was about him.
- **"Tat" means father** for everyone (Toril calls Hasha *Tat*, `:48`). For Kinora the word means her abuser.

## Ages and naming days

- **Kinora is eight or nine, small from neglect**, so a little younger than Tupa. Her naming day is still several years away. It falls in book 3, where she comes into her own (`plan/sequels.md`). Malena's guess of *"seven or eight"* (`~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md:520`) was too young, because she was going by Kinora's size.
- **Tupa's naming day comes in The Clanless.**

## Tupa's naming day is where she chooses (ruling, Q-M93X)

This replaces the Gemini plan's clan-assembly scene, in which *"Tupa publicly chooses"* (`prompts/synopsis-v8.md`, `prompts/outline-v1.md` chapter 31). The ceremony belongs to the clan and is held in Sotalio under Rovin's priest. Veshumi waits on the bank, and so does the fosterage their father arranged. In the water Tupa chooses a self-name, and the name itself is her choice of Malena and the clanless. Walking out of the river to Malena is what makes both of them clanless, so the label Rovin pins on them arrives at the same moment Tupa takes a name. Kinora watches from the bank, years from her own naming day. Veshumi's heartbreak happens at the water's edge, in public, where her respectability can't protect her. Rovin's ultimatum needs a new home, and it may follow from the naming itself, as the clan's answer to a child choosing against it.

## Tupa's two kinds of struggle (ruling, Q-P7VW)

- **The conscious dilemma: Father's wishes, or Malena.** Veshumi speaks for their dead parents, and Father arranged the fosterage at Erim's. Honoring the dead is what a good daughter does. Malena is the sister she trusts (*"Do you trust me?"*, `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md:741`). She feels this conflict fully, and her naming day decides it.
- **The confident blind spot: her father loved her well.** In death she idealizes him. Daniel describes him as *"shallow and self-interested and a poor husband to her mother"*. In canon he lectures the household about a grandson in front of strangers and treats the daughters' marriages as alliance-making (`:216`–`225`). Her loyalty to "Father's wishes" rests on a picture of him that isn't true. Malena and Veshumi both know better, and that raises a truth-telling question between the sisters: is it kind to let her keep the picture? The essay's *"what we deserve"* section applies to them.
- **Not a blind spot, but what she carries:** Shivi died in her place. That debt is real. It is a source of warmth (the warm coal), and it could tempt her toward a hard choice made out of guilt. It must not become the same blind spot as Oji's, earning a place by dying for it, because two characters with the same failure read as one character written twice (`~/code/me/novel-method/sources/harvest-g4.md`, G4-25).

## Kinora's two kinds of struggle `[?]`

**Withdrawn after Daniel's correction (2026-10-21).** The first proposal had Maco alive in the stepfather's house in Sotalio, and had her read Toril's silences as a coming blow. Daniel: *"Couldn't word be passed from Sotalio? And why would she not know that everybody outside the keep in Noemi had been slaughtered? And didn't her stepfather live there, rather than in Sotalio? ... Does she know, intellectually, that Toril is not her stepfather? She already likes him very much early in Cordimancy. ... I feel like we may be creating a caricature of a traumatized child, instead of one that's rich with an inner life."* Canon agrees with him. Noemi was *"wiped out"* (`~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md:1305`, `:1829`, `:2890`), and on the barge she already half-knows it (`:3528`). She already trusts Toril: *"Yes. He was one who would come. If."* (`:3530`).

**Her inner life, from canon.** She sings, and her voice is why Toril hired her (`:545`, `:3530`). She hums while she comforts another child (`:3520`). She names things: she named Cricket before she has a name of her own (`:2688`, `:3525`). She reads the quattroglyphs her dead father taught her (`:2497`). She makes hard choices under pressure and then second-guesses them. She left Cricket in the rushes to save him, and she asked about him first when she was rescued (`:3135`, `:3525`, `:4678`). She has a comic streak (*"cocked her eyes sideways in a comical gesture of calculation"*, `:536`). She came into Toril's house as hired help, an apprentice bargained for with her stepfather (`:3530`).

**What she carries into The Clanless.** She has to grieve Maco, Elesel and Cook. She also has to grieve a stepfather she is glad is gone, and to feel guilty about being glad.

**Daniel on who she is (2026-10-21):** *"this is one of the most important characters in the book. She's very intelligent, child-naive, but quick to see and learn. Her emotional journey matters to the book's title and premise."*

**Her confident blind spot: her place in this family is a job** (ruling, Q-GAPV). She trusts Toril and Malena to be kind. She doesn't trust that she is *theirs*. She came in as kitchen help, and the family grows around her in official ways: Tupa is Malena's blood, and Cricket and the baby are formally the couple's children. Daniel: *"it sets up a great and beautiful moment where Toril or Malena or both gently affirm that she is their child, not their servant. They should do it while defending her dignity or freedom in some way."*

**Her conscious dilemma `[?]`, in the direction Daniel gave:** *"a distant relative asserts 'ownership' of her, and she assumes she'll have to go, because she doesn't feel like she owns herself. When Toril gets angry, she assumes it's because he doesn't want to lose his servant girl, and is astonished when Toril goes to extreme lengths and high personal costs to protect her, and calls her his daughter. ... Could be Malena that does the defending. And Tupa could be involved (tells Malena that Kinora has consented and is on her way elsewhere, knowing more clearly than Kinora does how the two adults feel about her?)."*

The session's refinement:
- **The claim rests on Toril's own bargain.** Toril got her by bargaining with her stepfather for an apprentice (`~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md:3530`), and her wages went to that man (`:573`). With him dead, his kin inherit the bargain. Under Sotalio's law, with Rovin presiding, they hold her indenture, so the label *servant* is literally written into a contract. To call her his daughter, Toril has to undo a deal he made himself.
- **The claimant is trapped, not evil.** Noemi's ruin left the stepfather's people with no hands and a winter coming. To them a strong nine-year-old is survival, and the law says she is theirs.
- **Her choice, which she believes is right:** she consents to go. She is sparing Toril and Malena a fight they can't afford when they are already losing the clan, and she believes she owes it. It is the same act as leaving Cricket in the rushes: she lets herself go so the people she loves are spared. It is generous and wrong, and to her it is plainly right.
- **Tupa sees what Kinora can't.** She tells Malena that Kinora has consented and is already on her way. She knows how the two adults feel better than Kinora does.
- **Toril's blind spot meets hers.** His profile says that when a relative claims an orphan, *"he is certain he is protecting the child"*, and that part of what he protects is his own need (`plan/truth-profiles/toril.md`). In this scene his anger looks like possessiveness to Kinora, and perhaps briefly to the reader. What resolves both blind spots is that the defense is for her freedom, not for his keeping. They break the indenture first, so that she owns herself, and only then do they ask. She chooses them, and the word *daughter* is spoken after she has chosen.
- **It plants book 3.** *"With people, all true names are self-names"* (`:1445`). She can't choose a self-name until she owns herself. Her naming day comes in book 3, where she comes into her own (`plan/sequels.md`).
