# Truth profile: Desta

Each claim cites `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (cordimancy commit `64b2f69`), or quotes Daniel, or is marked `[?]` as the session's proposal.

## Daniel's statement

*"Corim's sister is referenced in 1 or two sentences in Cordimancy, and it explains who's been taking care of Cricket. Her willingness to come provides another solid adult anchor for the clanless group, and one not embroiled in all the complications that bog down Toril and Malena. She is just solid and good and sensible and competent."*

## Canon

- Corim: *"My sister left it here last time she came to visit."* (`:2986`). She doesn't live with him.
- Toril asked Corim to care for Cricket (`:3409`), and Oji left the boy at Corim's house with Hika (`:3450`). She is the one who has been doing the caring.

## Age

She and Corim are roughly a generation older than Toril and Malena (Daniel: *"Desta is older than Toril and Malena by roughly a generation, btw"*, *"(and so is Corim)"*).

## What she is not

She does not stand for *labor*. That word belongs to Malena, who has to find out what it means (`plan/brief.md`). The Gemini files cast her as *"practical, 'labor'-focused"* alongside Malena *"the 'Heart'"* (`prompts/malenas-arc.md:25`). Desta can be very good at hard work without being the word.

She is not Shivi come back, either. With Shivi and Paka dead, she is the only older woman in the group, and the reader will measure her against the grandmother Tupa lost (*"Is that what grandmas are like, Lena?"*, `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md:4727`). Shivi was wise, magical and mischievous, a teacher of forgiveness. Desta is sensible, practical and plain-spoken, and she has no magic. She shouldn't hand out wisdom, and nobody should call her grandma until she has earned it in her own way.

## Why she comes `[?]`

She has had Cricket for weeks, and she loves him. If Toril and Malena adopt him, he goes north into winter with them, and she can let him go or go with him. Coming is a choice she makes for him before it is a principle. That keeps her from being a saint, and it makes her goodness ordinary and easy to believe. She also has the one thing the group lacks: a sensible adult whose life hasn't just fallen apart.

## Two kinds of struggle, kept small `[?]`

She isn't embroiled, so her struggles are a size smaller than everyone else's, and that is part of what makes her restful company.

- **The conscious dilemma: her own life, or theirs.** She has a home and a trade somewhere that isn't Corim's house, and the exile costs her that. She weighs it plainly and chooses once.
- **The confident blind spot: anything can be managed.** She is sure that good sense, a list and an early start fix most things, and she is usually right. Grief, magic and Toril's pride don't yield to it. She learns to sit with what she can't fix. That is the patience in the Maxwell touchstone (`plan/touchstones.md`), learned by someone who never needed it before.

## How she tells truth `[?]`

Plainly and kindly, without spin. Every other adult in the group bends the truth in some characteristic way. She is the one who says the simple true thing, which makes her a foil to all of them. The essay's ideal speaker would sound like her (`~/code/me/sivanea.com/on-lying-and-truth-telling.md`).

## With Kinora `[?]`

Kinora named Cricket. Desta raised him after that. They are two gentle claims of love on one child. Desta is the first adult to tell Kinora, as plain fact, that Cricket is hers too. That is a small rehearsal of the moment when Toril and Malena call Kinora their daughter.
