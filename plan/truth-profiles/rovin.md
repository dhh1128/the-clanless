# Truth profile: Rovin

Each claim is marked *canon*, citing `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (cordimancy commit `64b2f69`), or quotes Daniel, or is marked `[?]` as the session's proposal. The brief says bad characters are *"mostly trapped in their own bad assumptions, not deeply evil"* (`plan/brief.md`).

## Daniel's skeleton

*"I think Rovin is personally ambitious/selfish, but the lie he believes is "I alone can fix this" (he is the most fit leader of the clan, and nobody else has good ideas). He believe this because it reinforces his preferred outcomes, perhaps. I also think he objectifies people, and that this is a grief to Veshumi. He is true to her in their marriage, but emotionally disengaged and not striving for an intimate, rewarding relationship."*

## Canon

- He heads another Kelun parijan. *"his ambition to become chief was well known. He was prosperous and carefully allied. Two of the parijans were in his pocket."* (`:307`)
- Hasha expected Gorumim to force the staff to Rovin, and outmaneuvered them both by passing it to Toril (`:306`–`310`, `:351`).
- Malena: *"he's insinuated himself into every center of influence, how he's managed potential adversaries. Not just recently, Toril—for years! He wants that staff."* (`:1710`)
- After Noemi he told the clan the osipi did it and sent men after them instead of after the children (`:1146`–`1147`). He claimed Toril used magic to force Hasha's story (`:1157`). He set up the priest who went to Sotalio (`:3333`).
- **He is competent in a crisis, and canon says so.** Vasari: *"Rovin steps into the power vacuum and acts decisively. He organizes defenses. People accept it."* (`:1305`). His lie has a true part, and that part is what makes it hold.
- Veshumi is married to his cousin, not to him (`:1710`).

## Two kinds of struggle `[?]`

- **The confident blind spot: I alone can fix this** (Daniel). The evidence he trusts is that it keeps working. The clan follows him, the defenses are organized, and Toril was off in the wilderness while Noemi burned. Each success confirms that the others are children who need a firm hand, and so he can't hear a good idea from anyone else.
- **The conscious dilemma: the war, or the truth.** Over the course of the book, evidence builds that Gorumim's border war rests on a lie: the rescued children, Malena's account, Kinora's testimony. Admitting it would save Kelun's men from Gorumim's army. It would also undo his standing, and in his own eyes it would leave the clan without a leader at the worst possible time. He feels both sides. His blind spot decides it for him: *a leader who admits error in a crisis loses them, and then who will hold them together?* He believes his silence is a sacrifice he makes for the clan.

## How he bends truth `[?]`

He tells people what they need to believe for their own good. The osipi story kept a frightened clan united, and he may half-believe it. By the essay's account (`~/code/me/sivanea.com/on-lying-and-truth-telling.md`), he lies sincerely: the lie serves an outcome he truly thinks is good, and it is also the outcome he prefers.

## He objectifies people `[?]`

He sees people as positions: allies, assets, problems. That shapes every scene that has been decided so far:
- **Kinora's indenture.** To him she is property under law, and a precedent. If an indenture can be broken by sentiment, he loses something he needs to rule. Toril's public confession that he bought a child is useful to Rovin, and he lets it happen.
- **The staff trade.** When Toril offers the staff in exchange for the freedom of every orphan and everyone who follows him, Rovin thinks he has the better of the bargain. He gets the staff, and Sotalio loses dissenters and mouths to feed before winter. He never understands what he sold.
- **The label.** He means *clanless* as a sentence that ends people. He can't imagine it becoming a name.
