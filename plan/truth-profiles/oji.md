# Truth profile: Oji (Oathizhi)

Each claim is marked *canon*, citing `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` (cordimancy commit `64b2f69`), or *proposal*, which waits for his correction. The arc comes from `plan/brief.md`: Oji *"finds a brother and a sister of the heart, and … this heals a hollow place in his heart."* The canon he stands on is `plan/backstory.md` OJ1 to OJ3.

## Where he stands (canon)

- He is a born osipi of thirteen, and an adult. *"Life is experience, stonecaster."* He will *"snuff suddenly, like a candle at its wax end. Is that such a bad trade?"* (`:2367`)
- He never chose his path, and he envies those who do: *"it would have been nice to declare an allegiance"* (`:2380`). And: *"Kindness has a high price, if it makes you become something you are not."* (`:2384`)
- He declared his allegiance anyway, twice: on the trail, choosing Toril, Malena and the children over his father's forgiveness (`:2735`), and in the palace, where the blow that sent Luim through the glass came the instant before he turned to the chained children (OJ1, OJ2).
- He keeps his own story to himself. He never said Luim was his father, or that he walks in aiki (OJ3).
- He calls Toril *"stonecaster"*.
- He has his own speech, which is canon: he fuses compound verbs. *"I day-live more than you week-live,"* *"word-put them,"* *"experience-trading,"* *"dotage-linger,"* *"record-fond"* (`:2330`–`2367`).
- At the end Malena understands: *"Oji, who had no family, no clan, and no magic except the pain of an aiki trance, who kept crouching between her and death—he was a heart, too."* (`:4745`)

## Label and name (proposal)

- **Labels pinned on him:** *almost*, *half-life*, *yolk-sucker* by humans (`:2057`); *traitor* by the ahu; and, in The Clanless, *parricide* by the starving refugees. He can neither confirm nor refute that last one (OJ1).
- **The name he holds:** the allegiance he declared, to the helpless and to the people who befriended him. That is close to the name Toril holds. Proposal: they become brothers of the heart *because they chose the same name*. Their brotherhood may be confirmed out loud at some point, in this book or in book 3, possibly formally, in a blood-brother rite (`plan/sequels.md`).

## Two kinds of struggle

- **The conscious dilemma: his people, or his chosen family, paid for with his own short life.** The osipi refugees are starving in the south, and they need him. The people who made his heart less hollow are going north into winter, and that is where he wants to be. Every aiki trance burns days off a life that is already short. So each rescue spends the very time he would have with the family he has just found. He feels every part of that.
- **The confident blind spot: belonging has to be earned by dying for it.** *"I will die today with a smile on my lips"* is how he understands his place: his worth to them is his blade and his readiness to spend himself. The reader can see that Toril and Malena want him *alive*, as he is, and that the hollow place won't heal while he keeps trying to pay for his welcome. Shivi's death gives him a model that is true and also dangerous. The book's turn for him is learning that costly love includes letting himself be loved, not only dying well.

## How he bends truth, and how he tells it (canon and proposal)

- **Bends it by withholding his own history** (canon, OJ3), and by bravado that turns injury into a boast (*"ten of your buddies"*).
- **Tells it as a blunt jab** (canon): *"You're a poor listener as well as a slow talker"* (`:2357`).

## What the reader should like (proposal, for REGARD)

He is funny and impatient, and he has the fastest mouth among the principals. He has the speckled lilies: he sees colors the humans can't (`:2365`–`2367`). He is plainly delighted to be alive, which makes the arithmetic of his short life hurt more.

## Where his arc goes (`plan/sequels.md`)

In book 3 Oji dies defending the people he loves from Gorumim's advancing soldiers, and Malena holds him as he dies and weeps for him. This shapes his blind spot here. Book 2 must not teach him that dying for them is wrong. In the end it is exactly what he does, and it is right. What has to change is *why* he does it. In The Clanless he learns to let himself be loved, so that his death in book 3 is a gift rather than a payment for a welcome he never believed he had.
