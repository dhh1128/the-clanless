# Truth profile: Malena

## Where she stands when The Clanless opens (canon)

- She **suspects she is pregnant, and is not sure**: *"For the thousandth time, she wondered if she was diagnosing its source correctly."* (4122). The Gemini dossier's claim that she *knows* goes further than the text.
- Her first worry about the pregnancy is other people's verdict on it, and she corrects herself: her parents *"would be humiliated"*, but *"their reaction paled in importance next to the one her husband might have"* (4122).
- **In her line, childbirth kills women.** Her mother: *"I put my mam in her grave, and my sister died in childbirth as well. Veshumi is still trying."* (224). She dreams of *"her aunt giving birth while her home burned around her"* (1955).
- She declared herself dead to Toril: *"You should have let me die, Toril. … it will not give you back your wife. There were no survivors here."* (1083).
- She carries survivor's guilt, which she names as cowardice: *"Why had she survived when they had not? Had her cowardice carried its own punishment?"* (1052).
- Her parents are dead. They are never found, and she knows it the way all three sisters know it (`plan/backstory.md` SH1). When Tupa tells her, she changes the subject at once (`:4695`–`:4696`).
- She grew up inside a marriage arranged for status. Her father wanted the line of Shavir restored to the chief's staff (224). Her mother's *"gaudy ruby"* heartstone held *"loneliness and self-doubt"*, and Malena *"allowed herself to hope for a better tenderness"*, found it in Toril's stone, and threw it away (4116).
- Shivi's definition of forgiveness was given to her: *"it chooses to love anyway"* (4327).
- She has dry wit even under mortification: *"Heaven's Fist! How many conversational firecrackers would her sister lob?"* (214).

## The goods she is torn between (proposal)

1. **Telling Toril the truth, against protecting what they have only just rebuilt.** The essay settles whether he deserves the truth: he does. So what she is choosing is *when* and *how* to tell him, and every delay is a lie of silence that she justifies as kindness.
2. **Love for a child she didn't choose, against revulsion at where it came from.** Neither feeling is wrong, and the book should never let one win cleanly.
3. **Hope, against a fear the family has earned.** Women in her line die giving birth, and the magical acceleration makes the fear sharper.
4. **The artist she meant to be, against the people who now need her.** The Gemini arc's "nurture is her true art" settles this too cheaply. Per the essay, she should be allowed to grieve the loss truthfully, without the plot paying it off.
5. **Keeping Tupa with her, against Tupa's safety.** Taking an eleven-year-old into exile because she belongs with love is a choice the reader may judge to be wrong.

## Labels (canon and proposal)

- **Labels she applies to herself:** dead ("no survivors here"), coward, soiled. Her arc moves away from the *victim* label along the essay's lines, with honesty about tense ("had been"), weight (a headline, or one line in her story), and her own power. **It never moves blame onto her.** The essay's three-month-old is the boundary: some victims had no volition.
- **Labels others will apply to her:** scandal, traitor's wife, Rovin's problem.
- **Labels the plan applied that should come out:** "the Heart", "Shivi-like", "Labor".

## How she bends truth (proposal grounded in canon)

- **Compliant phrasing that ends a conversation.** *"I know my duty."* (224) and *"I don't want to talk about it"* (1083). She says the acceptable thing so that nobody looks further.
- **Hiding care inside action.** Her first kindness to Toril is anonymous.
- **Minimization,** when the subject is her own body.

## How she tells truth (canon)

- Cornered, she tells it as an accusation and it cuts (1083: *"You were off slapping backs with your political buddies…"*).
- She trusts her own judgment against other people's logic, and the novel proved her right twice.

## Two kinds of struggle

- **The conscious dilemma: whether, and when, to tell Toril.** *"Malena is fully conscious of the conflict about the choice of whether to tell Toril. She feels very torn."* The reader suffers alongside her, and the scenes give her room to argue it with herself.
- **The confident blind spot: Tupa.** *"About Tupa, she's more confident and less self-doubting (and perhaps, more mistaken?)."* She takes Tupa into exile because she believes Tupa belongs with the people who love her. She doesn't agonize over it. Under strict third-person limited, the reader can see what she can't, for instance that Veshumi's house is warm and fed and the snow is not. The irony comes from her misreading of facts that both she and the reader have. This is also where the two sisters are most alike: each is certain she is protecting the youngest, and each is half lying to herself about whose need is being served. Whether she is in fact mistaken is left open, as his question mark leaves it.

## What the reader should like in her (proposal, for REGARD)

Her dry wit, her refusal to perform for anyone, her competence with her hands, and the fact that her first instinct after a cruelty, including one of her own, is to fix it.
