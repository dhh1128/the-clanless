# Brief: what The Clanless is for

These are the book's goals, in Daniel's words (`~/code/me/novel-method/runbook.md` A1). Every layer traces back to a goal stated here. Lines that cite `cordimancy.md` refer to `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` at cordimancy commit `64b2f69`. The prologue is `~/code/me/sivanea.com/cordimancy/bonus-prologue.md`. The quotations Daniel chose to set the book's tone are in `plan/touchstones.md`.

## What it is about

**The cost, the beauty and the sacrifice of costly loving relationships.** *"The Clanless is about the cost and the beauty and sacrifice for costly loving relationships. It should not be bleak."* The center of gravity is the Givens passage in `plan/touchstones.md`: to love is to become fully vulnerable, and the freely made choice to begin and sustain costly loving relationships is what makes love divine.

**The title's meaning inverts.** *"the people who are rejected by their clan cohere and form a clan of their own, defined by loyalty to a principle that none of them ever fully articulate but that boils down to love even at extremely high cost."* They are Gitám's people. He is the fifth god, *"the smallest finger on the hand, the Unpaired One who wandered mountaintops and adopted anyone the other Speakers didn't want … god of dregs, lost causes, broken pottery, and orphans"* (`cordimancy.md:193`). The book honors both chosen family and belonging and what choosing costs. Choosing makes Malena and Tupa *"essentially"* clanless: *"It's that 'essentially' that gives us a little freedom -- but the feelings of isolation need to be real."* Someone who chooses to stay inside the clan should be *"understandable, and heart-rending because we understand her."*

**The ordeal of names never ends.** All of life is the ordeal of names. Its power comes from freely choosing a wise name, one you choose for yourself rather than one someone else assigns, and then deliberately holding on to it. A name here is far more than a label, as it is in Hebrew culture: it captures a person's character and fundamental nature. Canon already says so:
- *"With people, all true names are self-names. … Names of great worth are forged at a price; the ordeal is to continue to choose the name when you understand its cost."* (`cordimancy.md:1445`–`1447`)
- *"Will you hold your name—be who you've chosen—even when all hope is lost?"* (`:1565`)
- *"You ask me to help you become. … But I cannot do so if I spare you the becoming."* (`:2536`)

**Heart magic over blood magic.** Cordimancy is an odd fantasy novel. There is no quest for a talisman, and the hero gives his magic away instead of using it. *"Shivi defeats the most powerful wizard in the world because Shivi's blood can't be bent to his intended use of blood magic. This is a test of wills, and names."* The Clanless keeps that shape: the power that matters is not the talent anyone was renowned for.

## The arcs, as Daniel states them

- **Malena** discovers that the name she once glimpsed in a quattroglyph *"is perhaps far more profound and desirable than she once credited,"* and that she wants that name intensely. She discovers *"that there can be joy after and even inside the pain."* Canon: her naming-day rocks said *labor* and *heart*, which she took for *"drudgery"* and swept away for rocks she liked better (`cordimancy.md:556`–`558`).
- **Toril** is *"forced to confront the cost of his idealistic choices, and groaning under the weight, but ultimately shouldering it with a will and finding joy and peace on the other side."*
- **Shivi and Paka:** *"their love doesn't stop giving, ever."*
- **Oji** *"finds a brother and a sister of the heart, and … this heals a hollow place in his heart."*

## What the reader should experience

- *"It is also a story, meant to be read and enjoyed."* That needs a rich milieu, characters the reader cares about intensely, and a huge emotional payoff at the climax.
- *"I want the reader to weep in frustration and worry as stakes mount, and weep with happiness when goodness is rewarded."*
- The book is not bleak. Cordimancy's foreword set the palette: *"You can't paint such things unless you have black on your brush—real black. … My brush also painted with as many bright colors as I could fit on the palette; the existence and beauty of such colors is at the heart of my design."* (`cordimancy.md:27`–`28`)

## The moral texture

- **No black-and-white good and evil.** *"I want the good characters to struggle to find the right way forward, and to have human frailties and foibles, and to misunderstand their own journey at times. The overcoming that they do has to be real."*
- **Moral complexity is gray choices, not gray intent.** *"a character that genuinely wants to be good, and that is genuinely likable, but that has to wrestle with competing values in tension … not grayness of moral intent, but grayness in the choices on the menu."* And: *"People … should have contradictions and should sometimes believe in the moral correctness of their choices even if the reader disagrees."* This is worked through character by character in `plan/truth-profiles/`, drawing on `~/code/me/sivanea.com/on-lying-and-truth-telling.md`.
- **Bad characters are mostly trapped, not evil.** *"I want bad characters to mostly be trapped in their own bad assumptions, not deeply evil."* Gorumim is the exception: *"We know enough of his backstory to understand his hatred and motives, but he's very far down the path in many ways."* His backstory is the bonus prologue (Moruvish, the dispossessed heir, and his clan-bond with the osipi).

## Ambition

This book is a step up from Cretaceous and Deep Resonance: *"even richer characters, a meatier ethical payload, lots of moral complexity, and nuanced human relationships along with a magic system and a world mythology."*
