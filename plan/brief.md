# Brief: what The Clanless is for

The book's goals, in Daniel's words where he has given them (`~/code/me/novel-method/runbook.md` A1). Every layer traces back to a goal stated here. Lines marked `[?]` are the session's proposals and wait for him.

## Stated by Daniel

- **Ambition.** A step up from Cretaceous and from Deep Resonance: *"even richer characters, a meatier ethical payload, lots of moral complexity, and nuanced human relationships along with a magic system and a world mythology."*
- **Moral complexity, as he defines it.** *"a character that genuinely wants to be good, and that is genuinely likable, but that has to wrestle with competing values in tension. This is a different kind of moral complexity -- not grayness of moral intent, but grayness in the choices on the menu."* And: *"People, in particular, I think should have contradictions and should sometimes believe in the moral correctness of their choices even if the reader disagrees."* This is worked through character by character in `plan/truth-profiles/`, drawing on his essay `~/code/me/sivanea.com/on-lying-and-truth-telling.md`.
- **Chosen family and belonging.** *"A big theme in this book is chosen family and belonging."* The title names the cost: choosing makes Malena and Tupa *"essentially"* clanless. *"It's that 'essentially' that gives us a little freedom -- but the feelings of isolation need to be real."* Someone who chooses to stay inside the clan, as Veshumi does, should be *"understandable, and heart-rending because we understand her."*
