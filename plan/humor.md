# L15 — Humor

Planning layer L15 (`plan/layers.md`). It records each character's comic register as Cordimancy shows it, the jobs comedy does in that book, and who carries the comedy now that Paka and Shivi are dead. L9 keeps the relief that isn't comic (ruling, Q-C0Q9). The governing idea comes from Cretaceous: *"Humor is characterization, never decoration"*, and *"the humor budget and the grief budget are the same account"* (`~/code/me/cretaceous/plan/humor-plan.md`, "The governing rule").

This is the **canon half** (ruling, Q-PR4D). Citations are to `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` at cordimancy commit `64b2f69`, and `prologue:` to `~/code/me/sivanea.com/cordimancy/bonus-prologue.md`. Placement waits for the scene list. The style canon's §12 (`prompts/style-canon.md`) has the prose-level tendency: comedy comes after the pain has been acknowledged, and nobody is mocked.

## What comedy does in Cordimancy

- **It sits against pain, on the near or far side of it.** The blackberry tarts come right after the stepfather (`:577`). The hat that sinks over Shivi's eyebrows (`:2403`–`2409`) comes just before Malena's sleepless dread that the child is not her husband's. Paka confesses a fatal lump and, in the same breath, *"I just about wet my pants. So much for stones"* (`:2625`). Comedy tends to go quiet inside the crisis itself: the climax (`:4552`–`4655`) has none apart from the villains' sneers.
- **It carries exposition.** Tupa's *"conversational firecrackers"* deliver the heartstone taboo and the pressure to bear a child (`:201`–`214`). Oji's *"I day-live more than you week-live"* delivers the osipi lifespan (`:2332`). Paka's sleight of hand with Hika leads into blood magic (`:1855`–`1890`).
- **It is how a good marriage sounds.** Paka and Shivi tease, snort and tug beards (`:1858`–`1871`, `:2408`, `:2816`, `:3230`). Their banter is the book's model of a marriage that has lasted, set against Toril and Malena's.
- **Between Toril and Malena, jokes fail.** Her candle-lighting story: *"Toril did not laugh, as she'd hoped"* (`:2199`). His admiring tease makes her flinch (`:2835`). *"Not even a smile, now and then?"* (`:4051`). The "potato man" reveal ends in an embrace she endures with her eyes shut (`:3299`–`3319`). Their one exchange that works is deadpan about the near-drowning (*"We could swim again"*, `:2832`). By the end, warmth comes back without jokes: a silence that *"felt warm, not strained"* (`:4706`) and the crinkle at his eyes (`:4752`).
- **Humor is offered as medicine, and can be refused.** Paka says outright: *"I was just teasin' to give her a bit of fun"* (`:1862`). Malena half-refuses it: *"A part of her resented the humor"* (`:1909`). Shivi glares down Paka's gallows joke about the Rift: *"Nobody felt like commenting"* (`:3425`–`3426`).
- **Laughter can wound and name.** First Man: *"I laughed because I thought the answer was obvious"* and *"All I did was laugh"* (`:1423`, `:1425`). Shivi's story against herself starts with laughing at Paka's basket (`:4311`). In the theology of the book, a laugh is a naming, and it can be a cruel one.
- **The antagonists' wit is contempt.** The clan chiefs' *"snide laughter"* (`:765`). Gorumim is the most sustained comic voice in the climax, and all of it is sadistic: *"all you really do is keep me amused"*, *"Don't begrudge me my drama"*, *"budgeting a pair of children"* (`:4483`, `:4488`, `:4490`). In the prologue his charm is a tactic (*"what he hoped was a humble smile"*, `prologue:71`). Toril answers him with laughter: *"unmistakably laughter"*, then *"Two lips. Two liars"* (`:4531`–`4532`).
- **The caper.** The jailbreak farce, with Paka directing (`:3165`–`3216`), Corim's potato cart (`:3254`–`3299`), and the ladder ruse in the city (`:4394`–`4398`). It is comedy of improvisation under threat, and the heroes are funniest when they are acting.
- **The dead stay funny in memory.** *"Paka with green feet and crumbs in his beard"* (`:4741`), callback to the frog song (`:1933`). Shivi's *"daisies that were really chickweed"* (`:4736`). The book ends by overturning a joke about hearts: *"Heart magic is The Five's way of laughing a bit, and keeping us humble"* (`:4740`).

## Who carries the comedy now `[?]`

These are proposals for the outline to test, not placements.

- **Corim takes Paka's register, and Desta answers it the way Shivi did.** This commits us to the premise that sibling banter can do the work Paka and Shivi's marriage did: an old pair who know each other's tricks, teasing in front of the young. It differs from theirs in one way that matters. It isn't a marriage, so it can't model one, and that job passes to Toril and Malena.
- **Toril and Malena's first shared laugh is a beat.** In Cordimancy their jokes fail. When one lands in The Clanless, it measures how far the marriage has come, and it can be placed. Because Toril is semi-mute, the joke that lands may be wordless.
- **Oji and the children carry the day-to-day comedy.** Oji's insults and delight, Tupa's questions and Kinora's calculations are canon registers that survive intact.
- **Comedy goes quiet in the crisis, as in Cordimancy, and returns as warmth and callback.** The book has deposits to draw on: the potato man, green feet, chickweed, Oji's nose, the honey cup.
