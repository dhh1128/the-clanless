# L3 — The band

Planning layer L3 (`plan/layers.md`). It tracks who is in the clanless group in each scene, when and why each person joins or leaves, and the unstated principle that holds them. The group is treated as a unit here, apart from its pairs.

Each scene gets two fields:
- who still feels outside the group, from inside it;
- whether a welcome in that scene reads as wages for usefulness.

This is the **canon half** (ruling, Q-PR4D). Citations are to `~/code/me/cordimancy/cordimancy.md` at cordimancy commit `64b2f69`. Placement waits for the scene list.

## The principle, and how it's carried

The brief: the rejected *"cohere and form a clan of their own, defined by loyalty to a principle that none of them ever fully articulate but that boils down to love even at extremely high cost"* (`plan/brief.md`). Two consequences follow:
- **The principle is enacted and not stated.** Nobody in the band explains what holds them. The one word they take, *clanless*, is taken by a child literally and without irony (ruling, Q-J9NP; `plan/labels-and-names.md`). This is negative space at the scale of the book (ruling, Q-ZN70): the band is carved out of what they do for each other, and nobody names it.
- **Isolation stays real** (*"the feelings of isolation need to be real"*). Belonging inside the band doesn't cancel what exile costs outside it.

## Canon's first band

Cordimancy's rescue party was a band before there was a word for it, and it set the terms:
- **Shivi and Paka joined uninvited, out of compassion,** and Toril wondered whether to let them (`:2372`–`2373`). Their reason, said after Nanar's story: *"Now you see why we have a soft spot for little ones … Frustrated nurturing"* (`:2811`).
- **Belonging without usefulness is canon.** Paka: *"Just because I'm not much good that way, doesn't mean you don't need me. We cordimancers have our uses"* (`:1916`). And *"We'd be worthless in a fight"* (`:1888`). This is the canon line against belonging as wages. It came from the warmest person in the book, and he is dead.
- **Oji joined by allegiance,** choosing them over his father's forgiveness (`:2735`).
- **Hika** stayed behind to guard Cricket (`:3450`).

## Who is there when The Clanless opens

- **Adults:** Toril, Malena and Oji (unconscious, `:4704`).
- **Children at the palace:** Tupa, Riva, Kinora, and the rescued children. Malena counted seventeen (`:4654`), then Oji brought five more, Kinora among them (`:4669`–`4675`).
- **At Corim's house in Two Forks:** Cricket, Hika and Corim's twin boarhounds (`:3450`, `:2934`–`2936`). Corim lives there.
- **The count** (derived, not stated): Gorumim paid for twenty-nine children (`:1197`). Twenty-two are rescued at the palace. Four died at Kirte (`:4242`). Cricket was hidden in the rushes (`:3135`). Tupa and Riva are named among the taken (`:1689`, `:4697`). If all three were among the twenty-nine, every child is accounted for.
- **Dead:** Shivi and Paka (`plan/cast.md`).

## Open

- **How many of the twenty-two rescued children are orphans,** and how many are claimed by surviving kin. The clan's claims on children are the indenture's pressure multiplied (L13).
- **When Cricket, Hika, the hounds and Corim's household join,** and how far Two Forks is from the road north.
- **Whether Vasari or the priest follow** (L13 has both staying for now, ruling Q-TNAC).
- **Who the second wave are.** The band gets one (ruling, Q-RJX0): others Rovin's clan rejects come to it as a refuge, so it grows from a family toward a clan, which book 3's settlement in the Rift needs (`plan/sequels.md`). The brief's *"the people who are rejected by their clan"* is plural.

## The two fields, for the allocation half

- **Who still feels outside.** Candidates from the profiles: Kinora, until *daughter* (Q-GAPV); Oji, who thinks belonging is paid for in dying (`plan/truth-profiles/oji.md`); Kael, among humans who are *"all the same"* to him (`plan/truth-profiles/kael.md`); Desta, the outsider who chose to come; Malena, inside her secret, until she tells Toril.
- **Welcome read as wages.** Flag any scene where someone is thanked or welcomed *for* a use: Kinora's work, Oji's aiki, Kael's sword, Desta's competence. The book's answer is a welcome given when the person is useless or a burden, as Paka was welcome (`:1916`). It must not be a scene where usefulness earns a place, because that confirms Kinora's and Oji's blind spots instead of answering them.
